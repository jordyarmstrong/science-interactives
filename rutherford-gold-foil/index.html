1Predict

In 1909 scientists pictured the atom as a plum pudding: positive charge spread thinly through the whole atom, with tiny negative electrons dotted through it. Alpha particles are tiny, fast and positive. What did Rutherford's team expect when they fired them at gold foil?

2Observe

Now switch to the Nuclear atom. This model gives what Geiger and Marsden really saw. Fire a stream, then fire 40 000 at real odds. What is different from the plum pudding atom?

"It was quite the most incredible event that has ever happened to me in my life. It was almost as incredible as if you fired a 15-inch shell at a piece of tissue paper and it came back and hit you."Ernest Rutherford, looking back on the experiment

3Explain

What does each result tell us about the atom?

Most alpha particles went straight through.

Most of the atom is empty space.

A few changed direction (were deflected).

There is positive charge in the atom that pushes the positive alpha particles away.

A very few bounced straight back.

The positive charge and almost all the mass are packed into a tiny, dense centre: the nucleus.

Think further: why was it done in a dark room?

Each flash on the zinc sulfide screen was tiny and faint. They counted the flashes by eye through a microscope.

Think further: why gold?

Gold can be hammered into very thin foil, less than a thousandth of a millimetre thick, so the alpha particles pass through only a thin layer of atoms.

Ernest Rutherford was born in 1871 near Nelson, Aotearoa New Zealand. In 1909 Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden did this experiment in Rutherford's laboratory in Manchester, England, and in 1911 Rutherford explained it with the nuclear model of the atom.
